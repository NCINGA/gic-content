Sri Lanka Police – General Information 
1. Introduction 
The Sri Lanka Police is the principal law enforcement institution responsible for maintaining law 
and order, preventing and investigating crime, protecting public safety, and enforcing the law in 
Sri Lanka. 
The Police provides services to the public through police stations, specialised units, and other 
police divisions. 
 
2. Main Functions of the Sri Lanka Police 
The main functions of the Sri Lanka Police include: 
●​ Maintaining law and order 
●​ Preventing crime 
●​ Investigating crimes 
●​ Protecting life and property 
●​ Maintaining public safety 
●​ Enforcing traffic laws 
●​ Supporting road safety 
●​ Receiving public complaints 
●​ Investigating missing-person cases 
●​ Preventing drug-related offences 
●​ Investigating cybercrime 
●​ Supporting the protection of women and children 
●​ Maintaining public order 
●​ Conducting investigations into reported offences 
●​ Providing assistance during emergencies and public safety incidents 
 
3. Police Stations 
Members of the public can visit the relevant police station to report incidents, make complaints, 
seek assistance, or obtain information about police services.

## Page 2

When making a complaint, it is useful to provide accurate information such as: 
●​ Name of the complainant 
●​ Contact information 
●​ Date of the incident 
●​ Time of the incident 
●​ Location of the incident 
●​ Description of what happened 
●​ Information about persons involved 
●​ Information about witnesses 
●​ Supporting documents or evidence 
 
4. Making a Police Complaint 
A person who has experienced or witnessed a crime or other incident requiring police attention 
can make a complaint to the relevant police authority. 
A complaint should contain accurate and clear information about the incident. 
Useful information may include: 
●​ What happened 
●​ Where it happened 
●​ When it happened 
●​ Who was involved 
●​ Whether there were witnesses 
●​ Whether there is any supporting evidence 
●​ Whether there is an immediate safety risk 
 
5. Emergency Situations 
In an emergency involving an immediate threat to life, property, or public safety, the public 
should seek police assistance through the appropriate official emergency channel. 
When reporting an emergency, provide: 
●​ Exact or approximate location 
●​ Nature of the emergency 
●​ Number of people involved 
●​ Whether anyone is injured

●​ Description of the immediate danger 
●​ Any other important information 
 
6. Reporting a Crime 
Members of the public can report suspected or actual criminal activities to the Police. 
Examples include: 
●​ Theft 
●​ Robbery 
●​ Assault 
●​ Threats 
●​ Fraud 
●​ Property damage 
●​ Missing persons 
●​ Drug-related offences 
●​ Cybercrime 
●​ Other offences 
The appropriate police authority will determine the relevant procedure based on the 
circumstances. 
 
7. Theft 
If property has been stolen, the incident should be reported to the relevant police station as 
soon as possible. 
Information that may assist the investigation includes: 
●​ Description of the stolen property 
●​ Identification numbers where available 
●​ Estimated value 
●​ Date and time of the theft 
●​ Location of the theft 
●​ Information about suspected persons 
●​ Photographs or other evidence 
●​ Witness information

8. Missing Persons 
If a person is missing, the matter should be reported to the Police as soon as possible. 
Useful information includes: 
●​ Full name 
●​ Age 
●​ Recent photograph 
●​ Last known location 
●​ Date and time last seen 
●​ Clothing worn 
●​ Physical description 
●​ Contact information 
●​ Any medical or other relevant information that may assist the search 
 
9. Police Clearance Certificate 
A Police Clearance Certificate is an official document relating to a person's police record and 
may be required for certain purposes. 
It may be requested for purposes such as: 
●​ Foreign employment 
●​ Visa applications 
●​ Immigration 
●​ Foreign education 
●​ Overseas travel 
●​ Other official requirements 
The current application process, required documents, fees, and processing requirements should 
be confirmed through the official Sri Lanka Police information. 
 
10. Traffic Law Enforcement

The Sri Lanka Police is responsible for enforcing traffic laws and supporting road safety. 
Traffic-related police activities may include: 
●​ Enforcing traffic regulations 
●​ Controlling traffic 
●​ Investigating road accidents 
●​ Detecting traffic offences 
●​ Supporting road safety 
●​ Managing traffic during special events 
●​ Taking action against dangerous driving 
 
11. Road Traffic Accidents 
When a road traffic accident occurs, safety should be given priority. 
Depending on the circumstances: 
1.​ Move to a safe location if possible. 
2.​ Assist injured persons when it is safe to do so. 
3.​ Seek emergency medical assistance when required. 
4.​ Notify the relevant police authority. 
5.​ Provide accurate information about the accident. 
6.​ Preserve relevant evidence where possible. 
Important information may include: 
●​ Location of the accident 
●​ Date and time 
●​ Vehicles involved 
●​ Injuries 
●​ Damage to property 
●​ Witness information 
●​ Photographs or videos where available 
 
12. Road Safety 
Road users should follow traffic laws and safety practices.

Drivers should: 
●​ Follow traffic signs 
●​ Obey speed limits 
●​ Maintain a safe distance 
●​ Drive carefully 
●​ Follow traffic signals 
●​ Avoid dangerous driving 
●​ Avoid driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs 
●​ Give appropriate consideration to pedestrians 
●​ Follow instructions given by authorised traffic officers 
 
13. Child Protection 
The Police has responsibilities relating to the protection of children from crime, abuse, 
exploitation, and other forms of harm. 
Concerns involving: 
●​ Child abuse 
●​ Violence against children 
●​ Sexual abuse 
●​ Child exploitation 
●​ Threats against children 
●​ Other offences involving children 
should be reported to the appropriate authorities. 
 
14. Protection of Women 
The Police deals with complaints involving offences and violence against women. 
Examples may include: 
●​ Physical violence 
●​ Threats 
●​ Sexual violence 
●​ Domestic violence

●​ Harassment 
●​ Stalking 
●​ Other criminal offences 
A person facing an immediate threat should seek police assistance without delay. 
 
15. Domestic Violence 
Domestic violence can involve harmful or threatening behaviour by a family or household 
member. 
It may include: 
●​ Physical violence 
●​ Threats 
●​ Psychological abuse 
●​ Sexual violence 
●​ Property damage 
●​ Other harmful behaviour 
Persons affected by domestic violence can seek assistance from the relevant police authorities. 
 
16. Drug-Related Offences 
The Sri Lanka Police works to prevent and investigate drug-related offences. 
Information relating to suspected illegal activities involving drugs can be reported to the relevant 
police authorities. 
The public should not place themselves at risk when reporting suspected criminal activity. 
 
17. Cybercrime 
Cybercrime refers to criminal activity involving computers, digital devices, online services, or the 
internet.

●​ Financial scams 
●​ Misuse of personal information

●​ Screenshots

●​ Transaction records 
●​ Bank or payment information 
●​ Website addresses 
●​ Social media account information 
●​ Telephone numbers 
●​ Other communication records

●​ Fraudulent accounts 
●​ Online threats 
●​ Harassment 
●​ Scams

22. Public Safety 
Maintaining public safety and public order is an important responsibility of the Police. 
Members of the public should report serious incidents that may pose an immediate risk to: 
●​ People 
●​ Property 
●​ Public places 
●​ Public order 
●​ Community safety 
 
23. Police Investigations 
When a complaint is received, the Police may conduct an investigation depending on the nature 
of the complaint. 
An investigation may involve: 
●​ Recording statements 
●​ Collecting evidence 
●​ Identifying relevant persons 
●​ Interviewing witnesses 
●​ Examining documents 
●​ Examining physical or digital evidence 
●​ Taking other lawful investigative steps 
 
24. Police Reports and Complaints 
When a complaint is made, relevant information may be recorded by the Police according to the 
applicable procedure. 
A complainant should keep any reference information provided in connection with the complaint.

This information may be useful when making a follow-up inquiry. 
 
25. Providing Information to the Police 
When providing information to the Police, members of the public should provide information that 
is: 
●​ Accurate 
●​ Clear 
●​ Relevant 
●​ Truthful 
●​ Supported by available evidence where possible 
False or misleading information may have legal consequences. 
 
26. Lost Property 
If a person loses important property or documents, they may contact the relevant police station 
and follow the applicable procedure for reporting the loss. 
Useful information includes: 
●​ Description of the lost item 
●​ Date and approximate time of loss 
●​ Location where it was last seen 
●​ Identification numbers 
●​ Photographs 
●​ Other identifying information 
 
27. Stolen Identity Documents 
If an identity document or other important document is stolen, the incident should be reported to 
the relevant authorities. 
The person may also need to contact the institution responsible for issuing the document.
